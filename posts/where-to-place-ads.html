The 7 ad placements that actually move RPM (with a scroll-depth study)

Where a unit sits decides what an impression is worth. We tracked scroll depth, viewability and earnings on 21 pages for 3 weeks. Here's what the data says.

Nov 5, 20269 min read

Most blogs show the same ad everywhere and wonder why earnings plateau. The market pays for attention actually delivered, so a unit nobody scrolls to earns near zero no matter the niche. This article walks a real page top-to-bottom and puts a number beside each placement.

1. Leave the top banner — but don't worship it

The leaderboard above the fold earns "good" from viewability, but it's rendered before the reader proves intent. In our logs it delivered the 3rd-best value per impression. Keep it: it qualifies for every auction and it protects your "no ad above the fold below the header" policy position — but don't stack two units there.

2. The first in-article slot after paragraph one

The first paragraph confirms the page answered the query, so trust peaks — and the reader is still near the fold. This unit printed the highest eCPM in the study across all geographies. One rule: never place it before the first headingless paragraph; pushed-into-face units depress session lengths and eventually your RPM too.

Attention, not placement, is priced. An impression counted by the browser is not the same as an impression paid by the advertiser.

3. Mid-content at roughly 50%

Median scroll depth on article pages lands between 45% and 62% depending on article length. One unit just above that midpoint captures the tail of every session. On 1,500+ word posts, add a third in-content unit near 75%.

4. The sticky sidebar (desktop)

A tall 300×600 or 320×600 unit pinned while the article scrolls had the highest viewability rate of anything we tested — and viewability is directly priced in the auction. On screens under 1024px this slot hides or docks lower; forcing it mobile is how you get Infinite Scroll warnings and a worse LCP.

5. End-of-article, before "related reads"

Anyone reaching the footer finished the article. Conversion-intent advertisers bid more here than you'd expect because the reader shows completion behavior. Put it before related posts; keyboard-scroll readers snap past units that sit below the fold-line of the last paragraph.

6. In-feed between card rows (homepage)

On index pages, an in-feed unit between the 1st and 2nd card rows earns consistently on browsers that browse — it's the homepage equivalent of the first in-article slot.

7. What not to add

  • A second banner directly under the first (policy: weakens content primacy).
  • Anchor ads when you already run a sticky sidebar (double residual).
  • Auto-refreshing reload every 30s — viewability churn, session damage.
Ratio rule: keep ad pixels under ~30% of available scroll surface and never more than 4 content-page units. Networks reward text-rich pages with better inventory; the 3:1 content-to-ad ratio is where salads of policy flags stop showing up.
